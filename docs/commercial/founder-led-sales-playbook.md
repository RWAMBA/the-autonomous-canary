# CanaryGuard Founder Led Sales Playbook

## Commercial objective

Secure three design-partner pilots, document measurable results and convert at least one pilot into recurring managed release support. Activity targets guide execution; they do not guarantee outcomes.

## Ideal customer profile

Prioritize B2B SaaS companies and software agencies with 5 to 30 engineers, GitHub Actions, Docker or containerized delivery, at least weekly releases and no dedicated release-governance team. The primary buyer is a CTO, Head of Engineering, Engineering Manager, DevOps Lead or Platform Lead.

Disqualify hobby projects, organizations without commercial releases, teams that do not use GitHub Actions, buyers seeking a complete CI/CD replacement and enterprises whose procurement requirements exceed the founding-pilot capacity.

## Offer ladder

1. Release-workflow review: 20 minutes, no fee.
2. Release-readiness assessment: USD 350, one repository, written gap analysis, no managed installation.
3. Founding release-governance pilot: USD 750, 14 days, one repository.
4. Post-validation implementation: test USD 1,500 to USD 3,000 based on written scope.
5. Managed support: test USD 400 to USD 1,000 per repository per month after value and support load are measured.

Prices after the pilot are experiments, not validated market rates. Quote only after discovery.

## Account scoring

Score each account from zero to two on GitHub Actions fit, release frequency, commercial software, visible DevOps or security concern and buyer accessibility. A total of eight to ten is priority A, five to seven is priority B and zero to four is nurture or disqualify. Record the public source and date for each non-obvious signal.

## Weekly operating rhythm

- Monday: research ten accounts and choose the week's 50-account working set.
- Tuesday to Thursday: send personalized first messages and conduct discovery calls.
- Friday: send due follow-ups, update next actions, review objections and improve one asset.
- Daily: check the protected customer-request queue and configured notification inbox; respond to qualified requests within one business day.

Weekly activity targets are 50 selected prospects, 40 personalized first messages, 8 to 10 follow-ups, 4 discovery calls and 1 to 2 proposals.

## Discovery call

Use 25 minutes. Spend five minutes on the current workflow, ten minutes on the cost and risk of the problem, five minutes on only the relevant CanaryGuard path and five minutes on the next decision.

Ask how often the team deploys, who holds final authority, which checks are mandatory, where evidence is reviewed, what happened during the last failed or rolled-back release, how long approval takes, whether the team can reconstruct a three-month-old decision, what data must never leave its systems, what would make a 14-day pilot successful and who approves a GitHub App installation.

## Proposal and closeout

Send a proposal only when the problem, repository, owner, installation approver, success criteria, timing and budget are known. At closeout, compare the recorded baseline with pilot results, identify risks found, confirm operational fit, recommend the narrowest continuation and ask directly for the paid next step. Request a testimonial and two introductions only after the customer confirms value.

## Pipeline definitions

- Researched: account and one current signal recorded.
- Ready: buyer or role, relevance and personalized opening recorded.
- Contacted: first message sent with date and channel.
- Replied: prospect answered.
- Discovery: call scheduled or completed.
- Qualified: problem, authority, repository and timing fit the offer.
- Proposal sent: written scope and price delivered.
- Pilot won: agreement signed and first payment received.
- Nurture: relevant but not ready.
- Closed: disqualified, declined or sequence completed without response.

Every active account must have one dated next action. No record may remain in Contacted, Replied, Discovery or Qualified without a next-action date.
